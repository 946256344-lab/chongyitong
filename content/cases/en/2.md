## IV. Prognosis Reference

Based on published evidence for canine hip dysplasia:

- **Total Hip Replacement (THR)**: >90% of dogs achieve good to excellent outcomes; long-term (5+ years) function approaches that of normal dogs
- **FHO**: ~70–80% of large-breed dogs achieve satisfactory results, though activity intensity and gait may be somewhat limited
- **Conservative management**: Effective symptom control, but arthritis inevitably progresses; surgical intervention is typically needed within 5–8 years
- **Untreated moderate HD**: ~40% develop significantly worsened pain within 5 years, requiring more aggressive intervention

> Age is a critical factor: THR at 2 years means the implant's expected lifespan covers most of the dog's life. Earlier intervention means less arthritis damage and better surgical outcomes.

## V. Questions to Ask Your Vet

1. Given the current level of arthritis progression, is there still value in conservative management as a bridge before surgery?
2. If we choose THR, how many procedures does your team perform annually? Which implant system do you use (BioMedtrix, Kyon, etc.)?
3. For bilateral HD, do you recommend treating both hips simultaneously or staged? How long between procedures?
4. What pre-surgical workup is required (cardiac evaluation, bloodwork)? Are there any contraindications to screen for?
5. What does the post-THR rehabilitation protocol look like? Is hydrotherapy or physical therapy available?
6. If we start with conservative management, can long-term NSAID use accelerate cartilage damage?
