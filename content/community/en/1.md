## Background

Kevin, 33, is a software engineer in Chengdu. His Corgi, Doudou, is 4 years old and weighs 12 kg — energetic, healthy, no prior issues.

On the evening of September 11, 2024, Kevin came home to find Doudou walking unsteadily, dragging his back legs. He assumed Doudou had tired himself out. By nightfall, Doudou couldn't stand at all. His hindquarters were limp. He dragged himself forward with his front paws.

Kevin rushed to a 24-hour emergency vet clinic at 11 PM. An MRI showed **T12–T13 disc herniation with spinal cord compression — Hansen Type I IVDD**. The emergency vet presented two options:

- **Surgery (spinal decompression)**: ideally within 48 hours; estimated cost ¥12,000–18,000
- **Conservative management**: strict crate rest for 4–6 weeks + corticosteroids; estimated cost ¥2,000–4,000

The vet said "earlier surgery means better outcomes" — but gave no specific numbers. Kevin, sitting in the waiting room, had no idea what to do. He submitted the MRI report and a description of Doudou's symptoms to Pet Med-Pal.

---

## Understanding the Report

Our specialist reviewed the MRI findings and explained the key clinical context:

**Why timing is everything in IVDD:**
- Hansen Type I disc herniation is common in chondrodystrophic breeds (Corgis, Dachshunds). It comes on fast and causes rapid spinal cord injury.
- If surgery happens **within 48 hours** of symptom onset: recovery of ambulation is approximately **85–90%**
- Beyond 48 hours — especially if deep pain perception has been lost — success rates drop to 30–50%
- Doudou was 18 hours in. **The critical question: does he still have deep pain perception?**

**Comparing the two options:**

| | Surgery (Spinal Decompression) | Conservative (Crate Rest + Steroids) |
|---|---|---|
| **Best for** | Deep pain intact, symptoms <48 hrs | Mild deficit (still walking), first episode |
| **Recovery rate** | 85–90% (within time window) | 50–60% (mild cases only) |
| **Risk** | Anesthesia, infection, ~5% surgical complications | Missing the surgical window; higher recurrence rate |
| **Recovery timeline** | 4–8 weeks of physical therapy post-op | 6 weeks strict crate rest |
| **Estimated cost** | ¥12,000–18,000 (incl. hospitalization) | ¥2,000–4,000 |

**Specialist assessment**: Doudou is 18 hours in, within the surgical window. Given complete hind limb paralysis, conservative management carries significant risk of permanent deficits. If deep pain sensation is still present, surgery is strongly recommended.

---

## Questions to Bring to Your Vet — Right Now

The specialist compiled these questions for Kevin to ask the emergency vet immediately:

1. Does Doudou still have deep pain sensation? (Pinch the skin between his toes firmly — does he react?)
2. What is the compression grade on MRI? Is there any T2 hyperintensity signal change? (Indicates hemorrhage or myelomalacia — critical for prognosis)
3. How many IVDD surgeries has the surgeon performed in Corgis specifically?
4. Is post-op rehabilitation available — hydrotherapy, acupuncture, electrical stimulation?
5. If we choose conservative management and he doesn't improve in 48 hours, is surgery still possible?
6. How soon can surgery be scheduled tonight?

---

## Outcome

Armed with the checklist, Kevin went back to the vet and asked about deep pain sensation. The vet confirmed **it was still present**. Surgery was recommended immediately.

The procedure started at 2 AM.

By day 5, Doudou's hind legs showed voluntary movement. By week 3, he could stand with support. By week 6, he was walking independently — with a slight wobble. After adding hydrotherapy and daily passive exercise, he was trotting around the neighborhood by month two.

Kevin wrote back:

> "If I'd hesitated that night — if I'd waited until the next morning — the window might have closed. Pet Med-Pal helped me understand what 'deep pain sensation' meant and why it was the only thing that actually mattered in that moment. That one piece of information is the reason Doudou walks today."
